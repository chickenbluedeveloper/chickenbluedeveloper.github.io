What's in it for me?

I started chickenblue and The Coop because I was annoyed.

In school we had English class, which I did not enjoy. We read books that were chosen by academics who would probably consider themselves high-minded. For context I grew up in Northern Ireland, our intellectual bench is not deep. The biggest casualty of our antiquated English syllabus was my love of writing. I used to fill books with silly adventures, quirky characters and childish fantasies. Five years of hard English stamped that out of me. Luckily twenty years later a global pandemic would strike and I would discover my love for it again.

First up was a film script. Certified rotten. Then I tried my first novel and fell in love with the characters I created and the world they occupied. Maybe I'll come back to it some day. It was my third and most recent project, another novel, that I thought had chops. It took a year to finish and another two to really polish. What I found out next is that the hours spent writing is the easy bit.

My book is fine. I'm under no illusions. Had Kate Bush read it as a child it wouldn't have inspired her to write an absolute banger. I tried it with publishers and heard nothing back. My beef, however, isn't with them.

You'll see it suggested a lot online as a springboard for aspiring authors, enter work in writing contests. Winning entries look great on cover letters. So short stories, flash fiction, poems were all entered and I heard zip back. I'm still under no illusions. There's a high probabilty these were garbage as well.

However I noticed a pattern. The indie contests, which should be accelerators for new writers, seem to almost exclusively be exploitative cash grabs that are won by people with a moderate Bluesky following. Worst of all, you never get to see the work that is deemed better than yours. I didn't enter these contests expecting feedback or validation, I entered to see how big the gap was.

So now you know my motivation, what's in it for me? REVENGE.

That's not true. This is annoying but I haven't lost any sleep over it. Although it did make me realise there's a gap in the market for a contest that, dreaming big, someday might garner enough credibility so some writer can mention winning it in their cover letter to a publisher and get them taken seriously. Everything after that is a bonus.
